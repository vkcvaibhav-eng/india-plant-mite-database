# Notes: AICRP Progress Report 2020-21 (XVII Group Meeting), survey chapter III.1

Source: `01d5e49b-Progress_Report_2020-21_-_AINP.pdf` (261 pp, text layer). Blocks H01-H07. Printed page numbers are as in the report.

## How it was transcribed
The report has a usable text layer, so the large Kalyani tables were parsed from the layout text with `scripts/tools/layout_table.py` (species | host habitat | distribution columns) and then checked by eye. Smaller tables (Navsari, Ludhiana, Jorhat, Thrissur, Kalyani's host-by-location tables) were typed in directly.

## What was recorded
- H01 Kalyani, West Bengal (printed pp. 137-162): rice, chilli, garlic, rose, vegetables, flowers, sugarcane, mulberry and the district tables for Nadia, North 24 Parganas, Murshidabad, Malda, Hooghly and Burdwan. Hosts and districts are separate lists, so rows are host_only and place_only.
- H02 Kalyani, Bihar surveys (printed pp. 163-172): fruit, vegetable, flower and miscellaneous crop tables for Muzaffarpur, Sitamarhi, Motihari and Darbhanga.
- H03 Kalyani host x location tables for chilli, garlic and mulberry (Tables 120, 121, 134).
- H04 Navsari, South Gujarat (Table 115).
- H05 Ludhiana (Table 117): Punjab and some localities of Himachal Pradesh and Rajasthan; GPS coordinates omitted.
- H06 Jorhat, Assam (Tables 158, 159).
- H07 Thrissur, Kerala (Table 160): 20 spider mite species on 31 hosts.

## Left out
- Bangalore (Tables 113, 114): species counts per family and host counts per species, with no host or place per species.
- Coimbatore: a list of species collected in Coimbatore District, with no hosts.
- Mashobra Tables 155 and 156: monthly predator counts in two apple orchards (predators not named beyond Neoseiulus spp.).
- Thrissur Table 161 (predatory mites on whiteflies), and the Rajasthan Petrobia latens distribution map.
- Relative abundance and diversity-index tables, GPS columns and figures.
- Some Kalyani rows whose columns were scrambled in the text layer (mostly Ascidae, Cunaxidae, Stigmaeidae and Tydeidae, which are outside the covered families) and Bihar predatory-mite rows where GPS text had mixed into the localities.

## Uncertain readings
- Host names are as printed, including local names (Kathbadam, Sadafull, Nayantara, Batabilabu). Standardisation is a later step.
- "Eotetranychus orientalis" in Navsari Table 115 is a misprint for Eutetranychus orientalis; it is recorded as Eutetranychus orientalis.
- Bihar rows from Kalyani have a Bihar state even though the centre is in West Bengal.
- Parsed rows lose the link between a species and a single host when the printed table gives lists; this follows the host_only/place_only rule used in the earlier layers.

## Repair of host lists (11 November 2026)
When host names were standardised, three faults in the Kalyani (H01, H02) host lists were found and repaired with `scripts/tools/clean_host_lists.py`:
- 18 rows were not host lists: relative-abundance counts and percentages and the Shannon, evenness and dominance rows. They are removed (observation rows 1,905 to 1,867).
- District and locality names had been read into the host column (the host and distribution cells of one row run together in the text layer). They are moved to the locality field.
- Host names split across cells ("Ash ... gourd", "Ladies ... finger", "pigeon ... pea") are rejoined, and tokens holding two hosts run together ("Hibiscus Lemon", "Brinjal Tomato") are split.
The before and after text of every changed row is in `data/review/aicrp_host_list_repairs.csv`. One row (Brevipalpus phoenicis, "gourd") had no host left and was removed.

## Mashobra Table 157 (added 11 November 2026)
Block H08 (pdf 186-192, printed 175-181), April 2020 to March 2021, typed from the text layer, which keeps one table row per line here. Same conventions as block I05 of the 2021-22 report: ERM = Panonychus ulmi, TSM = Tetranychus urticae, flat (red) mite = Tenuipalpidae, the pear rust mite of the text = Eriophyidae; yellow, black, red and unidentified mites are not entered.
Many rows of the 2021-22 Tables 132-135 are the same as rows here, word for word and count for count (Sundar Nagar, Madhorghat, Nirsoo, Kinnaur and Kullu orchards, Jachh in September, the Indora ornamentals), and the 2021-22 Table 132 is captioned "2020-21". The two blocks probably share part of one season's data, so do not add their row counts as independent records.
New host names: Kafal (Myrica esculenta), Buel (Grewia optiva, medium confidence), Reetha (Sapindus mukorossi).
