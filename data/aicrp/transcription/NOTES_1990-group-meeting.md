# Notes: AICRP First Group Meeting, December 1990 (survey chapter VII, printed pp. 139-154)

Source: `c28641c9-1990_first_group_meeting` (225 pp). Printed page = pdf page - 16. Blocks G01-G06. Transcribed from page images (pdf 155-171).

## What was recorded
- G01 Bangalore (pdf 155-156): species list with host and locality, 20 rows. One row (Eutetranychus orientalis) prints "several hosts / many places", so it has no host or place.
- G02 Kalyani Table 1 (pdf 159-163): 50 hosts with the mites found, no locality per row. The survey covered West Bengal, four Bihar districts and five Odisha districts, so the state is "West Bengal; Bihar; Odisha (not split)". Crop-age remarks kept in the remark field. Rows 47-50 are stored products (dried prawn, dried fish, papaya seed).
- G03 Kalyani stigmaeid and tarsonemid tables (pdf 164-165): host, places, month, slide number, with West Bengal places only. Multi-place rows use forced host x place pairing.
- G04 Ludhiana Table 5 (pdf 166-167): 26 species; hosts and localities are printed as separate lists, so they are stored as host_only and place_only rows.
- G05 Coimbatore (pdf 168-170): district tables for Tamil Nadu and Kerala, with host, mite and place. Family-only entries are kept as family rows.
- G06 Navsari (pdf 170-171): the text gives only families and host groups, so these are family-level host_only rows.

## Left out
- Pusa (VII.3): text only, 8 phytoseiid species not named.
- Varanasi and Hisar (VII.7, VII.8): "No report received" up to 8 December 1990.
- Chapter IV (resurgence of mite pests, printed pp. 99-130) is a literature status paper and spray experiments; chapter V is mites of honey bees; chapter VI and chapters VIII-IX are pesticide and other experiments. None of these is entered.

## Uncertain readings
- Ludhiana prints "Jammu" under H.P.; Jammu is in Jammu & Kashmir and is mapped there. Jakhal is left unresolved (it is in Haryana but listed among the Punjab surveys). The block state is "Punjab" for the Ludhiana rows, so rows with unplaced localities carry Punjab.
- Bangalore G01 includes Calcutta and Kerala as localities for rows in a Karnataka centre; kept as printed and mapped to their own states.
- Host spellings are kept as printed (Licopersicon, Zarbera, Galardia, Argimone). Standardisation is a later step.
- "Dolichotetranychus sp. nov." on coconut and "Eotetranychus strychnosi" are not in the Paper 1 checklist.

## Disease chapters I-III (added 11 November 2026)
Blocks G07-G12, read from page images. Only survey and natural-incidence records are entered; status papers (literature reviews) and spray trials are not.
- G07 Kalyani, litchi erineum (pdf 35-39, printed 28-30): the mite list from litchi (place not stated, January-August 1990); the Table A survey of Aceria litchii at Krishnagar (Nadia), Baruipur (24 Parganas) and Muzaffarpur (Bihar); the seasonal-incidence orchard at Mondouri (Nadia). Page 28 is scanned twice (pdf 35 and 37).
- G08 Pusa, litchi erineum (pdf 51-53, printed 40-42): Aceria litchii and eight predatory species on litchi in the RAU Farm orchards, October 1989-September 1990. "Amblyseius purni Gupta" is read as A. pruni Gupta. These predators are the same as Pusa's list in the 1990-91 report, so the two layers probably repeat one collection.
- G09 Kalyani, mango malformation (pdf 62-64, printed 50-52): Table J survey of Aceria mangiferae at Madanpur, Baruipur, Saptagram, Andul, Munkar, Chachol and Plassey; the mite complex of normal and malformed mango buds (place not stated).
- G10 Bangalore, pigeonpea sterility mosaic (pdf 81, printed 68): Aceria cajani at Bangalore (UAS campus) and Chitradurga, December 1989.
- G11 Coimbatore (pdf 98-99, printed 85-86): Aceria cajani in fortnightly sowings at the TNAU insectary, 1990.
- G12 Navsari (pdf 109-110, printed 96-97): Aceria cajani in staggered sowings and perennial pigeonpea; none on the weeds around the crop.
- Not entered: Pusa's pigeonpea plots and district survey (no mite and no disease found), Navsari's mango-bud observation (started August 1990, no result), the Bangalore mango work (not started), the oribatid identities "awaited from Z.S.I.", and the Cephaleuros alga host plants.
